Kicker: Software

Most internal tools are built for the person who *asked* for the tool rather than the person who will use it eight hours a day. Those are usually not the same person, and the gap between them is where bad software comes from.

The person who asked wants a dashboard. The person doing the work wants the thing to take four clicks instead of nine.

## Watch someone use it

Not a usability session. Just sit behind someone doing their actual job with the thing you built, and say nothing, which is harder than it sounds.

You will see them:

1. Keep a spreadsheet alongside your tool, because your tool won't let them note anything
2. Do a step twice because they can't tell whether the first one worked
3. Have a private keyboard shortcut for the bit you were proudest of

Every one of those is a bug report that would never have been filed. People don't report friction; they route around it and get on with their day.

## Make the common case fast and the rare case possible

The temptation with internal tools is to build for every case at once, because you can see all of them from where you're standing. The result is a tool where the thing done four hundred times a day costs the same effort as the thing done twice a year.

> Find the action that happens most often. Make it one click. Then make everything else merely possible.

The Quick Actions toolkit started as one button that did the single most common investigation step. It is still mostly that button. That's the version people use.

## Ship it unfinished

An internal tool has a captive, findable, patient audience of about nine people who will tell you exactly what's wrong within an hour. This is the best feedback loop in software and it is completely wasted by spending three weeks polishing before anyone sees it.
