Kicker: Music

There is a version of the pedalboard that is finished. I have believed in it for about eleven years. It sits at the end of a sequence of purchases, each of which is the last one, and once it exists I will stop buying pedals and start writing songs.

I want to be clear that I know this is not true. Knowing it has not helped.

## The shape of the problem

Every pedal solves a real problem. That's what makes it hard. The overdrive genuinely was too harsh. The delay genuinely didn't self-oscillate. Each purchase is individually defensible and collectively absurd, which is also true of most of the ways people avoid doing the work.

The thing a new pedal reliably provides is an evening. You unbox it, you patch it in, you play the same four bars for two hours, and at the end of the evening you have not written anything but you have *definitely* been doing music.

## What actually helped

Recording the first take. Not as a discipline or a rule — I'm not good at rules — but because a recording is the one thing a new pedal can't improve retroactively. Once the take exists, the pedal that would have made it better is just a pedal.

Most of what's in the Music section here is first takes. You can hear the room, and in one case you can hear a chair.

The board is currently eight pedals. It is not finished. It is not going to be finished. I've started to think that's the correct number of pedals to have.
